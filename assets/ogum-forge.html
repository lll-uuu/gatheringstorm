Ogum and his friends Alaká and Ajero went to consult the Ifá.
They wanted to know how to become the kings of their villages.
After a consultation they were instructed to make an ebó,
and Ogum was requested to use a dog as offering.
Time passed,
Ogum's friends became kings of their villages,
but Ogum's situatio remained the same.
Worried, Ogum once again consulted the Ifá
and the soothsayer said he should redo the ebó.
This time, he would have to sacrifice the dog over his own head
and spread the blood all over his body.
The meat should be cooked and consumed by all of his egbé.
After that, he should wait for the next rain
and look for a place where an erosion had occurred.
There, he should gather fine, black sand
and burn it in a fire.

Anxious for success, Ogum made the offering,
and, to his surprise, as he burned that sand
it transformed into a hot mass
that solidified as iron.
Iron was the hardest substance he has ever seen,
but it was malleable when hot.
Ogum started shaping the hot substance
First, Ogum made pincers
a tool to remove the hot iron from the fire.
This way it would be easier to manipulate the incandescent substance
Ogum then forged a knife and a machete.
Pleased, Ogum started making
all kinds of object with iron,
and taught others how to use them.
Abundance came to all.
From then on Ogum Alagbedé, the blacksmith, changed.
He prospered, and started being called,
"He Who Transforms the Earth into Money".

Prandi, Reginaldo. 2001. Mitologia dos orixás. With Pedro Rafael. Companhia das Letras.
