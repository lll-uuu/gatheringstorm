On the Earth created by Obatalá, in Ifé,
the orishas and human beings worked and lived as equals.
Everyone hunted and farmed using fragile instrument
made of wood, stone, or soft metal.
This is why work demanded great effort.
With the growing population in Ifé, food was scarce.
It was necessary to cultivate a larger area.
The orishas then gathered to decide what would they do
to remove the trees from the land and increase the area for farming.
Ossaim, the orisha of medicine, offered to go first
and clear the land.
But his machete was made of soft metal, and he didn't succeed.
Just like Ossaim, other orishas tried,
one by one, and failed
In the task of clearing the land for farming.
Ogum, who knew the secret of iron, had said nothing so far.
When all other orishas had failed,
Ogum took his iron machete, went to the bush and cleared the land.
The orishas, impressed, asked Ogum of which material
such a resistant machete was made.
Ogum then replied that it was iron
a secret received from Orunmilá.
The orishas envied Ogum for the benefits brought by iron,
not just to farming, as well as hunting and even war.
For a long time the orishas insisted with Ogum
to learn the secrets of iron
but he kept the secret to himself only.
The orishas then decided to offer him the kingdom
as long as he taught them
everything about such a resistant metal.
Ogum accepted the offer.
Humans also came to Ogum
to ask him for knowledge about iron
And Ogum gave them the knowledge of smithy,
until the day in which every hunter and every warrior
had their iron spear.
Despite having accepted the command of the orishas,
Ogum was first and foremost a hunter.
On a certain occasion he went out hunting and was gone for many days
in a difficult season.
When he came back from the bush he was dirty and ragged.
The orishas didn't like seeing him in that state.
They despised him and decided to dismiss him from his kingdom.
Ogum was disappointed in the orishas,
for when they needed his secrets of the smithy,
they made him king
and now said he wasn't worhty of ruling them.
So Ogum bathed himself,
dressed with palm leaves,
took his weapons and left.
In a distant place called Irê, he built a home
under the acocô tree, and there he remained.
The humans who received from Ogum the secret of iron
did not forget him.
Every December they celebrate the feast of Iudê-Ogum.
Hunters, warriors, blacksmiths, and many others
make sacrifices in Ogum's memory.
Ogum is the lord of iron, forever.

Excerpt from:
Prandi, Reginaldo. 2001. Mitologia dos orixás. With Pedro Rafael. Companhia das Letras.
