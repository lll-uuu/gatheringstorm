Tonight I dreamed that I was riding a strange kind of floating capsule that never stopped. It just went around Rio, my hometown, forever. In order to get supplies we needed to hop off at certain train stations (which looked like the regular train stations in the North part of the city). The capsule slowed down at these spots, to allow people to hop in and out, but did not stop. The capsule was the only safe place, I remember being told, but somehow when I went to pick up supplies outside, I didn't feel unsafe. I picked things up from the ground or bartered in a rush—bananas and cassava, I think,—then went back to the platform to hop on the large, slowly moving capsule.

On the horizon I saw a storm gathering, and that felt like true danger—much more than the abstract danger that being outside of the capsule supposedly carried. The heavy clouds had an odd hue, an artificial magenta, as if filtered through a computer screen.

Strong winds, unpredictable and threatening, whipped my loose hair around, covered my eyes. As I got back on the capsule, the clouds were forming into a massive storm, looming darkly over the Pão de Açúcar mountain, enveloping it in a pink and purple mist.

When the rain came, it felt like the ocean and the sky had swapped places, the combined power of Iemanjá and Iansã whipping away at every surface, every crevice. We heard on the radio that the Christ statue, the symbol of the city, was about to be toppled by the force of the rain and the winds. I ran to the window and looked outside: there, in the distance, the great mountain was completely enveloped by the mist; behind it, in between whisps of fog, i saw the immense shadow of the statue slowly tipping over, as if time had slowed down. A great rumble was rising, a drum roll in anticipation of the end.
